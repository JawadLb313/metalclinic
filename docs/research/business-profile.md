## 0. Method & reliability caveat (read this first)

This session's network egress proxy **blocks direct access** to `google.com`,
`instagram.com`, `facebook.com` and `themetalclinic.com` (HTTP 403 on CONNECT).
Every fact below therefore comes from **search-engine indexed copies** of those
pages, not from the live pages themselves.

What that means practically:

- Text-based facts (bio, founder story, credentials, phone, email) are **reliable** —
  they came back consistently across multiple independent queries.
- Numbers that change over time (follower count, review count, star rating) are
  **as-indexed**, possibly stale by weeks or months.
- Anything not indexed by a search engine — the Google Maps address string,
  opening hours, star rating, photos, Instagram post content, pricing — is
  **simply missing**, not absent from the real listings. See §6.

Anything below marked **[unverified]** or **[gap]** must be confirmed with the
owner or by opening the pages in a normal browser before it drives a decision.

## 3. Services, as currently described

From the website's own wording:

- **Customized programs per individual**, covering fitness, general health and
  nutrition, aimed at the client's self-imposed goals.
- Science-backed methods **per ACSM standards**.
- **Readiness screening**: prospective clients are welcomed *on the premises or
  online* to screen their readiness for exercise and physical activity.
- Programs are *"tailor made to meet set targets according to the highest
  international training and nutrition quality standards."*
- Delivery of the coaching itself: design exercise programs to goals/needs;
  demonstrate and explain new exercises; supervise and spot clients during
  movements; maintain records of client progress; educate clients on health
  and fitness.
- **Both in-person and online coaching** are offered.

Note the phrase *"on their premises"* — it implies a **dedicated training space**
(a private studio) rather than a coach who floats between commercial gyms. That
is consistent with there being a Google Maps pin at all. Confirm with the owner **[unverified]**.

## 4. Channel-by-channel audit

### Website — themetalclinic.com
- Only **two pages surfaced in search indexes**: the homepage and `/terms-of-service/`.
- No services page, pricing page, booking page, blog, testimonials page or
  contact page appeared in any query. Either they don't exist or they are not
  indexed — both are problems, and both point the same way.
- Terms of service are **governed by the laws of Lebanon**; the payment,
  cancellation and booking clauses could not be read **[gap]**.
- Homepage title tag: *"The Metal Clinic Fitness Coaching – by Alain Eid"* — good,
  it disambiguates from metalwork on the SERP.

### Instagram — @themetalclinic
- ~**910 followers / ~190 following / ~71 posts** **[as indexed]**.
- Bio is a **credentials list only** — no location, no call to action, no offer,
  no "DM to start". The bio is doing zero conversion work.
- Post content could not be read **[gap]** — content themes, posting cadence,
  reel vs. static mix, and engagement rate are all unknown and matter a lot.
- 71 posts across ~6 years of operation is a **very low publishing rate** (roughly
  one post per month). For a coaching business whose main acquisition channel in
  Lebanon is almost certainly Instagram, this is the single most striking finding.

### Facebook — facebook.com/themetalclinic
- Page exists. Indexed description reads along the lines of *"health & wellness is
  our priority, we help achieve fitness goals."*
- No address, phone, hours, category or follower count was retrievable **[gap]**.
- Appears to be a **secondary, low-activity channel** — nothing in the index
  suggests it is actively maintained.

### Google Maps
- A verified place entity exists (stable Place ID `/g/11xtqpsrx1`), so the listing

## 5. Discoverability findings (the most actionable part of this research)

These are observations from running ~10 real search queries, not speculation.

1. **The business is effectively invisible in Lebanese local search.**
   Queries for gyms/personal trainers in Antelias, Bsalim, Rabieh, Metn and Beirut
   returned competitors repeatedly — and never The Metal Clinic. It is absent from
   every Lebanese directory that ranks for those queries: `whereleb.com`,
   `yelleb.com`, `lebanondaleel.com`, `fitlynk.com`, `onlifelebanon.com`.
   Free directory listings are low-effort, high-yield fixes.

2. **The name actively fights the business.** Searching "The Metal Clinic" collides with:
   - metal fabrication / metalwork businesses,
   - a Spanish furniture account (`@metal.clinic`),
   - medical clinics (the word "clinic"),
   - and — worst — **"Lebanon" as a US place name**. Searches consistently returned
     gyms in Lebanon TN, PA, OH, OR, MO and NH. Every generic query about this
     business gets hijacked by American towns called Lebanon.
   Implication: the brand cannot rely on name recall alone. It needs to own
   *"personal trainer Metn/Antelias"*-type intent, not *"metal clinic"*.

3. **The website is not carrying its weight.** A homepage plus a terms-of-service
   page is a business card, not a funnel. There is no indexed page that answers
   *what does it cost*, *where exactly are you*, *how do I book*, or *who has this
   worked for*.

4. **No pricing is public anywhere.** Not on the site, not in social, not in any
   directory **[gap]**. Whether that's deliberate (quote-on-request) is a question
   for the owner — it's a legitimate strategy, but it should be a choice.

5. **Local competitive set** (nearby, similar positioning — for benchmarking, not exhaustive):
   - *The Fitness Clinic* — Dbayeh (closest name collision **and** closest geography)
   - *Fitness & Beyond* — Mazraat Yachouh (same village cluster as the pin)
   - *GYM BOX* — Zouk Mosbeh, explicitly markets as "your personal training studio"
   - Plus the aggregators above, which own the top of most local SERPs.

## 6. Open questions — things only the owner can answer

Ranked by how much they'd change a plan:

1. **What is the goal?** More clients, more online clients, or a better-looking
   brand? These lead to different work.
2. **Exact address + opening hours**, and is the space private-studio or shared?
3. **Google Business Profile**: is it claimed? What is the current rating and
   review count?
4. **Pricing and packages** — per-session, monthly, online tier, nutrition-only tier?
5. **In-person vs. online split today** — where do clients actually come from now?
6. **Current client count and capacity** — is the constraint demand or hours?
7. **Website platform** — who built it, is there CMS access, is the domain/DNS in hand?
8. **Instagram**: who posts, how often, and what's the real engagement? Any Insights
   screenshots would replace all the guesswork in §4.
9. **Booking flow today** — DM, WhatsApp, phone? Is +961 3 402 413 a WhatsApp number?
10. **Language** — is the audience English-first, or does Arabic/French content matter?
11. **Any other names in the business** — additional coaches, or is it Alain solo?

## 7. Read of the situation (opinion, clearly labelled)

The substance is strong and the packaging is weak. A founder with a
100-pound personal transformation, an 11-year corporate backstory that mirrors his
likely client (desk job, weight creep, doctor's warning), and a genuinely stacked
certification set — ACSM, ACE, Pn1 — is a better story than most Lebanese
coaching brands have. What exists online communicates almost none of it: a
two-page brochure site, a credentials-only Instagram bio, ~71 posts in six years,
a dormant Facebook page, a thin Maps listing, and zero directory presence.

The cheapest wins, if and when work starts, are almost certainly in this order:
**(1)** fill out the Google Business Profile and start collecting reviews,
**(2)** rewrite the Instagram bio into an offer with a call to action,
**(3)** add the free Lebanese directory listings,
**(4)** then rebuild the website around the founder story, a clear offer, and a
booking action. A site rebuild first, with nothing behind it, would be the
expensive way to fix the cheap problem.

None of this is committed to. It is where the evidence points.
